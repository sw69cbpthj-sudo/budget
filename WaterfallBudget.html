Waterfall Budgeting

Money flows like a waterfall — it fills the most important bucket first, then the next, and the next, until it runs out. You never have to guess where money goes.

1. Groceries and Fuel — always first
2. Urgent debts and arrears
3. Savings transfer
4. Regular bills and spending
5. Fun money — only if there is any left
Getting Out of Debt

The waterfall method works for debt because you put every extra dollar at the highest priority debt first. Once that's gone, that money cascades to the next debt.

1
List every debt by urgency
Overdrafts and late fees come before everything else — they grow fastest and hurt your credit most.
2
Pay minimums on all, attack one
Every extra dollar above minimums goes to Priority #1 until it's gone. Then that payment amount moves to Priority #2.
3
Set a weekly transfer amount
Even $25/week to savings prevents the cycle of borrowing for unexpected expenses.
4
Track it by week, not month
Paychecks are biweekly. Thinking in weeks makes it easier to match income to expenses and spot leaks faster.
Handling Unexpected Expenses

Something always comes up. The plan is: which savings bucket can cover this? If no savings exists, the app will show you a week-by-week plan to build up to that amount.

When you have no savings

Log the expense as Unexpected. The app will calculate how many weeks of your current savings transfer it takes to cover the amount — and flag it in your plan.

1
Log it immediately
Don't ignore it. Logging it shows the real impact on your Checking 1 balance right now.
2
Pull from the right savings bucket
Emergency Fund first. Then the goal with the least urgency. Never pull from mortgage buffer unless it's for the mortgage.
3
Make a payback plan
Add a priority item to replenish the bucket you pulled from, with a weekly amount and a target number of weeks.
Saving by the Week

Monthly goals feel abstract. Weekly goals are actionable. Every goal in this app has a weekly contribution amount. Here is the math that matters:

1
Mortgage buffer: $244/week
Goal is $2,927 (one month's mortgage). At $244/week that's 12 weeks to never pay late again.
2
Emergency fund: $25/week
Even small amounts build the buffer that stops the debt cycle. $25/week = $1,300/year.
3
Kids activities: save in advance
Soccer, camp, back-to-school — set aside the weekly amount now so the lump sum isn't a crisis.
The most important rule

Pay yourself first — even $10 to savings before bills. A tiny savings balance changes how you make decisions. Zero savings = panic spending.
